The pitch, in three sentences

Nothing to compromise. Nothing to subpoena. Nothing to harvest.

01 / Identity

No account to compromise.

There's nothing to sign up for because there's no service to sign up to. Your identity is a cryptographic key that lives on your device. If nobody has your data, nobody can leak it.

02 / Infrastructure

No server to subpoena.

We don't run a central server. The architecture doesn't have one — it can't. There's no single point to compel, compromise, deplatform, or shut down. The protocol outlives the people running it.

03 / Metadata

No metadata to harvest.

Messages travel through Tor. Contact exchange happens out of band. Even the infrastructure that helps deliver offline messages sees ciphertext addressed to a hash — never people talking to people.

How it works

A message's journey, without a middleman.

When both parties are online, messages flow directly peer-to-peer over Tor. When the recipient is offline, an optional mailbox — which you run, a friend runs, or a volunteer runs — holds the ciphertext until they come back. No operator ever sees contents, sender identity, or who the recipient really is.

What's encrypted, what isn't.

Message contents, group metadata, attachments, and reactions are all sealed inside MLS before they leave your device. Only group members can decrypt them — not the mailbox, not the network, not us.

What the mailbox actually sees.

A ciphertext blob and a recipient hash — no plaintext, no sender identity, no social graph. Self-host your own on a Raspberry Pi and even that limited visibility belongs to you.

Signal is an excellent app for most people. If "strong end-to-end encryption with a mainstream UX" covers your threat model, use Signal — we're not trying to replace it. Skattr is for the cases where "strong end-to-end encryption" isn't enough and you need stronger metadata guarantees, no phone number tied to your identity, and no central service in the loop.

Trust, calibrated

What we claim. What we don't.

Privacy apps that overclaim age badly. Here's the honest split between what the design actually guarantees and what remains your responsibility.

What we claim

  • +
    Message contents stay between you and the recipient.

    End-to-end encrypted with MLS. Nothing in the middle — not the mailbox, not the network — ever sees plaintext.

  • +
    Network observers can't see who you're talking to.

    All traffic goes over Tor v3 onion services. Your IP doesn't leak to the recipient; theirs doesn't leak to you.

  • +
    No central service holds your identity or social graph.

    There is no central service. Identity is a keypair on your device. Contact discovery doesn't happen through anyone.

  • +
    Forward secrecy and post-compromise security.

    Past messages stay private even if keys are later compromised. Groups recover security after a member is removed.

  • +
    Open source and reproducibly built.

    Verify that the binary matches the public source, or build it yourself. Nothing hides in release artifacts.

What we don't claim

  • −
    We don't call it "anonymous."

    Your mailbox operator knows when you come online and how often. We say metadata-minimizing and pseudonymous — those we can defend.

  • −
    We don't protect against a compromised device.

    If someone has your unlocked laptop, they have your messages. We can help with panic-wipe and encrypted storage — we can't defeat physical access.

  • −
    Deletion is cooperative, not enforced.

    "Delete for everyone" works if the other client honors it. Like every other E2EE app, we can't reach into someone else's device.

  • −
    We're new and not yet audited.

    Pre-1.0 is experimental. An independent audit is scheduled before the first stable release. Until then, don't use this for anything that really matters.

  • −
    We're not trying to replace Signal for most people.

    Signal is mature, excellent, and right for most threat models. Skattr is for the cases where Signal isn't enough — not the cases where it already is.

Design rules we hold ourselves to.

Every decision in Skattr traces back to a short list of rules. When the rules conflict with convenience, the rules win — because the convenience wouldn't matter if the privacy properties didn't hold.

No custom cryptographic primitives. No "minor tweaks" to established protocols. No features that require a central trusted party. No telemetry, ever. No dark patterns, especially around security-critical flows like seed phrases and contact exchange. Honest docs — including this page — about what the system does and doesn't protect.
